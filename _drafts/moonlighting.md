## 

Soon after the covid19 pandemic, when remote work became the new norm, the [/r/overemployed](https://www.reddit.com/r/overemployed/) subreddit gained prominence. People were posting crazy, outlandish stories of holding down multiple full-time jobs (all remote), doing the bare minimum, collecting simultaneous paychecks, and even getting away with it for months (and years).

This phenomenon of "over-employment" was a constant source of amusement to me.

Until.

Sometime in 2022, while serving as a fractional CTO for an early-stage startup, I had a new employee (industry hire, senior developer) pull this on us.

This over-employment went on for about four months. Not entirely undetected; there were tell-tale signs: Doing the bare minimum. Skipping meetings. Not responding to emails. Not showing up for calls. Not taking the lead on anything. 

The HR department was able to collect evidence using EPFO (Employees Provident Fund) records, although I'm still hazy on the exact details. Ultimately, this led to the employee being terminated. But till date, I have no idea whether the HR department notified the other three companies where this person was simultaneously employed. 😄
