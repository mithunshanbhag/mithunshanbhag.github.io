## ✅ The over-employment phenomenon

Soon after the Covid19 pandemic, when remote work became the new norm, the [/r/overemployed](https://www.reddit.com/r/overemployed/) subreddit exploded in popularity, seemingly overnight. People were posting crazy, outlandish stories of:

1. Securing multiple, simultaneous full-time jobs (all remote)
2. Doing the bare minimum at each
3. Collecting simultaneous paychecks
4. And getting away with it for months (and years)

Peak insanity! This was fast becoming a worldwide trend in the information-work and knowledge-work sectors; the only necessary condition being that the jobs had to be remote.

The stories on that subreddit were a constant source of amusement to me.

Until.

## ✅ Someone actually pulled this on me

Circa early 2022, I was serving as a fractional CTO for an mid-stage startup. Like everyone else at that time, we had transitioned into a fully-remote, fully-distributed workforce. We had just hired a new remote employee: an industry hire, senior software developer.

I was in the remote interview loop, and was pretty impressed with the candidate: technically sound, easy going, and a seemed like a good culture fit. The overall consensus was a strong "hire". Then the HR department did their standard background verification. No red flags. The references checked out too. We rolled out the offer letter, and the candidate accepted and joined us. Off to a great start!

Things started off great! The employee's engineer manager was enthusiastic: __**"He looks very promising"**__.

A few months went by, and the enthusiasm had waned a bit: __**"He's doing all right"**__.

A few more months went by and there were mentions of performance/delivery gaps. But the manager was still gung-ho about handling this situation, giving the employee the benefit of the doubt, chalking up the gaps to __**"He's still acclimatizing to our engineering processes and tech stack"**__.

One fine day, someone from payroll flagged discrepancies in this remote employee's EPFO (Employees Provident Fund) records. Apparently, the employee was also simultaneously working full-time for three other companies (side note: I'm hazy on the exact details now, but seemingly multiple companies were simultaneously linked to the employee's EPFO UAN).

After this, I asked the HR department to intervene: they started a formal investigation, collected evidence and eventually terminated the employee.
