## The over-employment phenomenon

Soon after the Covid19 pandemic, when remote work became the new norm, the [/r/overemployed](https://www.reddit.com/r/overemployed/) subreddit gained prominence. People were posting crazy, outlandish stories of:

- securing multiple, simultaneous full-time jobs (all remote)
- doing the bare minimum at each
- collecting simultaneous paychecks
- and getting away with it for months (and years)

Peak insanity! What started of as a trend soon became a worldwide phenomenon in the information-work and knowledge-work sectors; the only necessary condition being that the jobs had to be remote.

The stories on that subreddit were a constant source of amusement to me.

Until.

## Someone actually pulled this on me

Sometime in early 2022, while serving as a fractional CTO for an early-stage startup, we had a new employee: an industry hire, senior software developer.

Ultimately, this led to the employee being terminated. And till date, I have no idea whether the HR department notified the other three companies where this person was simultaneously employed. Perhaps he's still working for all of them! 😂
