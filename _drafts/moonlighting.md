## ✅ The over-employment phenomenon

Soon after the Covid19 pandemic, when remote work became the new norm, the [/r/overemployed](https://www.reddit.com/r/overemployed/) subreddit gained popularity, seemingly overnight. People were posting crazy, outlandish stories of:

1. Securing multiple, simultaneous full-time jobs (all remote)
2. Doing the bare minimum at each
3. Collecting simultaneous paychecks
4. And getting away with it for months (and years)

Peak insanity! What started of as a trend soon became a worldwide phenomenon in the information-work and knowledge-work sectors; the only necessary condition being that the jobs had to be remote.

The stories on that subreddit were trending on `/r/all` and were a constant source of amusement to me.

Until.

## Someone actually pulled this on me

Sometime in early 2022, while serving as a fractional CTO for an early-stage startup, we had a new employee: an industry hire, senior software developer.

I was in the remote interview loop, and was pretty impressed with the candidate: technically sound, easy going, and a seemed like a good culture fit. Then the HR department did their standard background verification. The references checked out too. We rolled out the offer letter, and the candidate accepted and joined us.


@TODO: But something was not quite right. The employee was not performing as expected. There were delays in deliverables, missed deadlines, and a general lack of ownership.

@TODO: This over-employment went on for about four or five months. Not entirely undetected; there were tell-tale signs: Doing the bare minimum. Skipping meetings. Not responding to emails. Not showing up for calls. Not taking the lead on anything.

Once I asked the HR department to intervene, they were able to collect evidence using EPFO (Employees Provident Fund) records, although I'm still hazy on the exact details. Ultimately, this led to the employee being terminated. And till date, I have no idea whether the HR department notified the other three companies where this person was simultaneously employed. Perhaps he's still working for all of them! 😂

## Calculated mediocrity

We thought it was: distracted, lazy, unmotivated, incompetent. Maybe they had a family situation, some personal issue, or health scare. 

We kept giving them the benefit of the doubt.

Mental gymnastics to rationalize the situation..

Reflecting back, the tell-tale signs were all here. We just never connected the dots.

bare minimum effort, doing just enough to get by, not taking ownership, not showing up for meetings, not responding to emails, not taking the lead on anything. Being reactive and not proactive.

![calculated mediocrity](../../../images/moonlighting-calculated-mediocrity.jpg)

His manager (whom I'm friends with, and is probably reading this) has the attention span of a goldfish.
