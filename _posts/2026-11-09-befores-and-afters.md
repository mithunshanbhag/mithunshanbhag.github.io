The simplest way for a brand to tell a story is to show a "before" and an "after" that are opposites of each other, with the brand effecting this change.

Let's create a simple "before vs after" story for a fictional local neighborhood record store 'GrooveNest'.

With short illustrated stories, there is no time for exposition. The first panel must do the setup; it must visually indicate, very clearly, the main character(s) in a distressed or less than ideal state. In this particular example, our sleep-deprived subject is clearly feeling lethargic and groggy.

The second panel does the switch/transition. This is usually where the brand comes in to save the day. The brand mascot Rory, plays some upbeat music to lift the tempo of the room. Subject starts tapping her foot to the music.

The third panel is the payoff. The main character is now in a significantly better mood.

To be honest, this comic strip doesn't even need dialogues or speech bubbles.

And technically speaking, even the mascot is not really needed in these scenes. But mascots and embossed logos help the readers in recalling the brand instantly.

What's the "before vs after" that **YOU** strive to create for your customers? And how do you help them visualize it?
