On April 17, 1973, **FedEx** created history by successfully executing doorstep pick-up and overnight delivery of 186 packages. Operations spanned 25 U.S. cities, 14 planes, and 389 employees. Many things could have gone wrong logistically, but [FedEx delivered on its promise](https://www.fedex.com/en-ps/about/company-info/history.html).

In 1989, **Hampton Inn** told guests that if they were unhappy with their stay, they wouldn’t have to pay for the room. Ten years later, Hampton reported that it had provided [more than $6 million worth of free rooms under that guarantee](https://hotelonline-assets1.nyc3.digitaloceanspaces.com/wordpress/hotelonline-News/News_posts/PressReleases1999_4th/Oct99_HamptonGuarantee.html).

Want to hear a story about a brand keeping someone else's promise? You read that right! In 1975, a man rolled two used tires into a **Nordstrom** store in Fairbanks, Alaska. He’d driven more than 50 miles. The retailer that used to occupy that location had sold him the tires and promised he could return them anytime. Nordstrom associate Craig Trounce knew very little about tires, so he called a tire company, found out what the pair was worth, [and paid the man that amount](https://press.nordstrom.com/news-releases/news-release-details/nordy-pod-truth-about-nordstroms-legendary-tire-story). What??? 😄

I want to storyboard a "promise kept" story for "Lunch Tables" - a fictional catering business. Their promise is simple - timely lunch delivery by noon.

Let’s storyboard this from the workshop organizer’s point of view. The story beats are as follows:

1. Panel 1 (the setup): Four minutes to go. The organizer sits at her laptop, chin in hand. Behind her, the attendees are still taking notes. “I hope lunch will be ready by 12 PM.” We put the clock in the panel so you can see exactly how little time she has. The dominating emotions here are worry and anxiety.

2. Panel 2 (the switch): A message from the caterer. She checks her phone. “Lunch will be ready at 12 PM.” She smiles. That text does one small but important thing for her: reassurance. With four minutes to go, she doesn’t have to chase someone down to find out what’s happening.

3. Panel 3 (the payoff): It's noon now! The clock reads 12:00 PM. The lunchroom doors are open, the trays are laid out, and the Lunch Tables mascot welcomes everyone in. The organizer gets to say the one line she’s been hoping to say all along. “Let’s break for lunch!”

![a promise kept - full comic](../../../images/promise-kept-1.jpg)

It's the first two panels that actually tell the story - A worry, followed by reassurance from the brand. The trays of food in the final panel visually reinforce the promise kept.

Think about a promise your customers count on you to keep. If you had three panels to tell that story, what would your customer be worrying about in the first one? And what would they see in the last?
